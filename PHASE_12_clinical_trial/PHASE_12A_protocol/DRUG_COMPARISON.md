## Key Differentiators for Febuxostat

### 1. Mechanism of Action
- **Current biologics:** Block downstream inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, interleukins)
- **Febuxostat:** Directly stabilizes NOD2 LRR domain, addressing upstream genetic defect

### 2. Route of Administration
- **Current biologics:** IV infusion or subcutaneous injection (requires healthcare setting or self-injection training)
- **Febuxostat:** Oral tablet, once daily (convenient, no injection burden)

### 3. Cost
- **Current biologics:** $20,000-$30,000+ per year
- **Febuxostat:** <$1,000 per year (generic available)
- **Potential savings:** >95% cost reduction

### 4. Safety Profile
- **Febuxostat:** 10+ years of post-marketing safety data in gout patients
- **Known risks:** Cardiovascular events (FDA boxed warning in gout), hepatotoxicity (rare)
- **Monitoring:** Liver function tests, cardiovascular risk assessment

### 5. Precision Medicine
- **Current biologics:** No genotype-based patient selection
- **Febuxostat:** First genotype-stratified therapy for Crohn's
  - Include: WT, R702W, G908R, N852S, M863V (pocket intact)
  - Exclude: L1007fs (pocket deleted)

## Unmet Need

Despite multiple biologic options, significant unmet need remains:
- ~40% of patients do not respond to first-line biologics
- ~50% lose response over time
- High cost limits access globally
- No therapies target the underlying NOD2 genetic defect

**Febuxostat represents a potential paradigm shift:** targeting the root cause (NOD2 dysfunction) rather than downstream inflammation, with genotype-guided patient selection.
